The Forum Cycle

One cohort, from the moment a stranger buys a seat to the Tuesday night their guests walk into graduation — and how that night writes the records that fill the next Forum.

The Forum runs Friday to Sunday and graduates on the Tuesday evening. Everything below is keyed to that week, because every record in the system is written on one of those days — and the last two days of the cycle are the ones that fill the next one.

Three objects carry the whole thing. An Invitation records how someone arrived. A Contact records where they are in the journey, in one glance. A Registration records one course engagement in full. The Forum registration is the master: it holds the participant’s own course detail and a mirrored block for whatever they sign up for next, so the portal and every email template can render the next step from a single record.

STAGE 01The master is born

A stranger buys a seat. Everything downstream hangs off the single record that moment creates.

The purchase and the registration are two different events, and they do not arrive in a reliable order — on the two live purchases on 20 Aug, the post-purchase webhook fired 17 seconds before the registration existed on one, and 19 seconds after it on the next. So the purchase door stays deliberately thin, and the registration-created rule is the anchor. It is also the only trigger all three course types share: Forum, Advanced Course and Seminar every one of them creates a record on object 10001, while Seminars never produce a purchase at all.

The one thing that must be true here

f2424 Registration Status is blank on every newly created Forum record. Every workflow that looks for a participant’s master registration filters on Active, so those records are invisible to the Advanced Course and Seminar stampers — and they are invisible to exactly the people who registered most recently.

Writing f3374 Forum Registration ID onto the contact at this stage removes the search altogether: later stages follow a pointer instead of guessing.

STAGE 02The portal opens, two days out

From here until long after graduation, the participant sees the system through one page, and that page reads one record.

The portal does not assemble a view from the contact, the event and the registration. It reads the master Forum registration and renders what it finds. That is the reason the master carries mirrored blocks for the Seminar and the Advanced Course rather than pointers to them — a card that needs a second lookup is a card that does not render.

It is also why the event fields matter more than they look. The moment f2214 Event is set, the graduation day and date, the graduation times, the time zone, the start date, the countdown reference and the course all arrive on the registration as related-data mirrors, at no cost. Confirmed live on registration 1325. Never compute a date the event already holds.

STAGE 03 & 04Saturday and Sunday: two records dock onto the master

Seminar registration opens on the Saturday of the Forum. The Advanced Course is announced on the final day. Both create their own record, and both stamp a summary block back onto the master.

The split matters. The new Seminar or Advanced Course registration is the source of truth for that engagement — it owns its event, its dates, its money and its attendance. The block on the master is a mirror for display, and it exists purely so the portal and the email templates can render the participant’s next course without a second lookup. Change the AC dates and you change them on the AC record; the stamp then republishes them onto the master.

Keep the branches as separate workflows

Publishing is a discrete step on this account. A single monolithic router means a change to the Seminar branch forces a republish of the live Forum path. Three sub-workflows behind one shared head and one shared tail costs nothing and isolates the blast radius.

STAGE 05Sunday to Tuesday: the participant becomes a source

This is the hinge. Between the last day of the Forum and graduation night, participants invite people, and every invite writes an Invitation record.

An Invitation is the entry point object: who invited them, to which event, and what that invitation eventually produced. It is written once, at the start, and never rewritten. 376 exist today; 281 carry an inviter and the rest are guests who registered themselves.

The rule that makes the cycle measurable is credit the source event, always. A guest invited at event 218 who ends up registering for the November Forum is a registrant of 283 but a conversion of 218. The scope is the invitation’s f2258 — never the event they eventually joined. Break that and the Forum that did the work gets none of the credit.

STAGE 06Tuesday night: two populations in one room

Graduation is where the participant cohort and the guest cohort meet, and it is the last moment before the system has to tell them apart.

Everyone in the room holds a record, but not the same one. Participants hold a master Forum registration. Guests hold an invitation. Attendance is written in both places: f3210 Attended on the invitation, and f3361 Attended Graduation on the contact. The second one is what the offer funnels read the next morning.

STAGE 07Wednesday morning: four buckets

From Tuesday through the following Saturday, everyone who touched the cycle is in exactly one of four offer tracks. The bucket is decided by two questions: were you a participant or a guest, and are you already a graduate of the thing being offered?

The word graduate means something different on each row, and that is not sloppiness — it is the actual segmentation. For a participant, the question is whether they have taken the Advanced Course. For a guest, the question is whether they have ever completed the Forum.

Every one of those four definitions is a read of the contact journey layer. That is the reason the journey flags matter well beyond reporting: f3359 to f3363 are the audience definition the next sale runs on. A journey flag nobody writes is a funnel that targets nobody.

And the flags must be derived, never typed, and monotonic — set, never cleared. Completed Forum stays true forever. Withdrawal and refund are registration-level facts; they do not un-happen a journey step.

Where the segmentation stands today

Across 667 contacts: f3359 Registered For Forum is set on 113. f3360 Completed Forum is set on 102 — exactly the event 218 completer count, so it was backfilled once from that one cohort and never maintained since. f3361, f3362 and f3363 are set on 0.

Two of the four buckets above currently resolve to an empty audience.

STAGE 08The loop closes

Each bucket produces a different record, and every one of them re-enters the pipeline at the door it came from.

READ IN ONE PASSWhere the cycle is broken today

Five of the eight stages work. Read against the map above, here is what each stage is actually doing right now.

  • Stage 01f2424 Registration Status is blank on every new Forum registration. The record exists and the portal renders, but every workflow that filters on Active is blind to it — including the Seminar and Advanced Course stampers.
  • Stage 02Portal and pre-Forum sequence: working. Access grant, countdown and confirmation tracks are all live.
  • Stage 03Seminar: working end to end. 75 registrations, all Active, 75 masters stamped. No seminar SKU exists in the 16-product catalogue, so zero money on all 75 is correct by design — not a backfill gap.
  • Stage 04Advanced Course: dead at the door. Both creation workflows are inactive, so 29 purchases have produced 10 registrations and 19 are unclaimed. The stamper downstream is active but never called.
  • Stage 05Invitations are created, but conversions are not marked. 376 records exist; f2299 is set on 2, f2298 and f2300 on none. The cycle runs, but it cannot be measured.
  • Stage 06Graduation attendance never reaches the contact. f3361 is set on 0 of 667 contacts, and automation 97 is named for exactly this job.
  • Stage 07Two of the four buckets resolve to an empty audience, because the flags that define them are written on nobody.
  • Stage 08Every Forum purchase reaches an Advanced-Course-shaped filter and is discarded. Automation 15 posts to a workflow that tests the product name for “Advanced” and drops everything else into a node named Abandon — Not Advanced Course.

The order that unblocks the most

  1. Set f2424 = 154 Active at registration creation. One change; it fixes the Forum records and unblocks the Seminar and Advanced Course master lookups at the same time.
  2. Build the router on the registration-created trigger, classifying from f3080_id, with three branches and a shared tail. Build the sweepers alongside it, not after — one door is one point of failure.
  3. Turn Advanced Course registration creation back on, de-hardcoded from event 218, then backfill the 19 unclaimed purchases. Check contact 915 first: it holds two $850 AC purchases, and a naive backfill would create two registrations for one person.
  4. Backfill the journey layer from the registrations that already exist, then let the router maintain it. This is what switches the two empty buckets back on.
  5. Repoint automation 15 at a thin purchase handler that writes contact state only, and stop testing product names to decide who a sequence applies to.
One rule that keeps the handoffs clean

One explicit signal per course type. The router sets the contact journey flag and applies a start tag; the welcome track subscribes to that and nothing else. A sequence must never do its own product or event lookup to decide whether it applies — that is precisely how the current catch-all ended up testing product names inside a webhook and discarding every Forum buyer.
